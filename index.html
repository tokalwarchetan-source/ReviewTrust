The problem

A single review is easy to fake.
A pattern isn't.

Individually, a fabricated review can look perfectly ordinary. It's only when you line up timing and connections across many reviews that the coordination becomes visible.

01

Bursts hide in plain sight

Bulk campaigns land in a tight window — dozens of five-star reviews in an hour — then go quiet for weeks.

02

Rings share more than opinions

Coordinated accounts overlap on the same small set of products, devices, or posting rhythms far more than chance allows.

03

Text alone isn't enough

Language models make polished fake reviews trivial to write. Wording checks catch less and less on their own.

How it works

Two signals, fused into one score

ReviewTrust scores every review on when it happened and who it's connected to, then combines both into a single suspicion score.

Signal 01

Temporal analytics

  • Burst detection against an expected posting rate (Hawkes / Poisson baseline)
  • Change-point detection on sudden rating or volume shifts
  • Account age vs. time-to-first-review
  • Purchase-to-review gap, where verified-purchase data exists
Signal 02

Network analytics

  • Dense bipartite cores in the reviewer–product graph
  • Community detection to surface reviewer rings
  • Graph embeddings feeding an anomaly classifier
  • Shared device / IP clustering, where available
Architecture

From raw review to action

A five-stage pipeline turns a stream of reviews into a ranked, explainable suspicion score.

STAGE 1

Ingest

Reviews, timestamps, reviewer and product IDs, ratings, and text arrive as a stream.

STAGE 2

Temporal engine

Scores each review against expected posting-rate baselines and recent change points.

STAGE 3

Network engine

Builds the reviewer–product graph and flags unusually dense or star-shaped clusters.

STAGE 4

Fusion score

Combines both signals into one suspicion score with an explainability trace.

STAGE 5

Action

Routes high-confidence rings for review and feeds outcomes back into the model.
